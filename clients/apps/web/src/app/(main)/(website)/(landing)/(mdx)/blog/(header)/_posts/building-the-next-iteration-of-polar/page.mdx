Billing was built for a world where a customer was a person, a product was a seat and a price was set once a year. That world is going away.

### Billing is changing

**Every request costs.** Inference puts a real cost behind every action. Margin moves per customer, per model and per function invocation. Yet most billing remains blind to it, and only finds out at the end of the month.

**Teams are not all humans.** One customer can now spawn any number of agents and automations acting on its behalf. Each of them needs its own spending, its own credits and its own guardrails.

**Billing is now in the hot path.** Entitlement and credit checks run before every usage event. That makes billing latency product latency. A slow check is a slow product.

We think metering, entitlements and billing are really one problem. So we're building one substrate for all three, and it's the next iteration of Polar.

### Where it started

These ideas started at our team offsite in Berlin. With everyone in one room, we asked ourselves a simple question. If we were designing billing from scratch today, for products that are consumed by the token, what would we build?

![The Polar team in discussion around a table at our offsite in Berlin](/posts/blog/building-the-next-iteration-of-polar/team-berlin.jpg)

The answer came from long discussions around the table, from pulling apart the assumptions every billing system is built on, and from noticing that metering, entitlements and billing kept collapsing into the same problem.

### From prototype to validation in San Francisco

An idea that survives an offsite is still just an idea. We wanted to know if it held up outside our own heads.

So we built a working prototype and took it to San Francisco. We pitched it to startups, scaleups and enterprises, the teams that feel the limits of today's billing the most.

![Three members of the Polar team in San Francisco, with the bay and Alcatraz behind them](/posts/blog/building-the-next-iteration-of-polar/san-francisco.jpg)

The response was overwhelming. We came home with more love, support and feedback than we could have hoped for, and much of that feedback is already shaping what we build next.


Here is what we're building. Some of these ideas we're confident in. Others we expect to argue with once more customers are running on them.

### Zero latency metering

In order to make zero latency metering a reality, we had to make the following a reality.

#### Everything is an event

Usage, payments, credit grants, entitlements and inference costs are all recorded as events. Together they form one history of each customer that you can audit and reproduce. Every number you see traces back to something that actually happened.

**Checks that answer instantly.** Knowing whether a customer can keep going shouldn't slow your product down. Balances and entitlements are available right where your product runs, so a check adds no noticeable latency, even when an agent makes thousands of them.

**One source of truth.** Usage, credit balances and business metrics all come from the same events. They can't drift apart, because they were never separate to begin with.

### Pricing confidence and velocity

**Unit economics built in.** Cost is recorded right next to revenue. You can see what each customer, each request and even each feature actually earns you, as it happens.

**Business guardrails.** Set the rules that protect your margins, and they apply before the cost is incurred. If a customer drops below the margin you want, move them to a cheaper model. If credit use suddenly spikes, slow it down.

**Branches and simulations.** Test new pricing against your real usage history before you ship it. You see what every customer would have paid, and what you would have earned, so a pricing change stops being a leap of faith.

### Billing as code

**Your business, defined as code.** Your pricing, meters and entitlements live in your codebase, next to the product they describe. They're reviewed in pull requests, versioned with your product and deployed like everything else, whether the change comes from you or from one of your agents.

**Built for your editor.** Your editor knows your pricing. It autocompletes the events and meters you've defined, and a typo or a malformed event is caught before it ever reaches a customer, instead of surfacing on an invoice.

### What comes next

Our mission is simple to say and hard to build. Billing should be a function of events. Usage goes in, revenue comes out, and what every customer has used, is entitled to and costs to serve is known the moment it happens.

We're early. We'll keep writing about what we learn, what we get wrong and what we ship, in plain words. If you're building an AI product and billing is already holding you back, we'd love to hear from you.
