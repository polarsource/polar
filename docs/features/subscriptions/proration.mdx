When a subscription changes price mid-cycle, part of the current period is already paid for. **Proration** is how Polar settles that difference: charge it now, add it to the next invoice, or skip it and apply the change at renewal.

It applies to plan changes and to seat and unit changes. You set a default for your organization and can override it on each update. The default for new organizations is `prorate`.

## Proration behaviors

The dashboard labels below are the options under **Settings → Subscriptions → Proration** and in the **Update Subscription** modal.

| Behavior | Dashboard label | Change applies | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `invoice` | **Prorate & charge now** | Now | Charged or credited now |
| `prorate` | **Prorate on next invoice** | Now | Added to the next invoice |
| `next_period` | **Schedule for next cycle** | Next cycle | None |
| `auto` | **Prorate upgrades, schedule downgrades** | Upgrades now, downgrades next cycle | Upgrades charged now |
| `reset` | **Charge full amount & reset cycle** | Now, with a new cycle | Full new price charged now |

### Prorate and charge now (`invoice`)

The change applies now and Polar invoices the prorated difference right away. An upgrade charges the customer. A downgrade credits them.

Use it when money should move at the same time as the change, for example to collect upgrade revenue now.

### Prorate on next invoice (`prorate`)

The change applies now, and the prorated difference is added to the **next scheduled invoice** instead of being charged now. The billing cycle doesn't change.

The customer sees their plan change at once and the adjustment on their regular renewal invoice.

<Note>
  If the billing interval changes, for example monthly to yearly, `prorate` becomes `invoice`. The old cycle has no next invoice to add the difference to.
</Note>

<Warning>
  With `invoice` and `prorate`, the change only applies if the immediate payment, if any, succeeds. If it fails, the API returns an error and the subscription stays as it was.
</Warning>

### Schedule for next cycle (`next_period`)

The change doesn't apply now. Polar stores it in the subscription's `pending_update` and applies it at the start of the next billing period. No proration charge or credit is issued.

A new update always replaces a pending one. If you schedule a `next_period` change and then update with `invoice` or `prorate`, the pending change is dropped and the new one applies now. To drop it without another change, see [Clear a scheduled change](/features/subscriptions/manage#clear-a-scheduled-change).

Use it for downgrades when you don't want to issue credits, or whenever the current period's terms should stay as they are.

### Prorate upgrades, schedule downgrades (`auto`)

<Note>
  Available from API version 2027-01. See [Update Subscription](/api-reference/next/subscriptions/update-subscription). The CLI and the `2026-10` SDKs don't accept `auto`. You can still choose it as the organization default in the dashboard, and agents using the MCP server can pass it.
</Note>

Polar decides per change. **Upgrades** apply now like `invoice`, and **downgrades** wait for the next cycle like `next_period`. Customers can upgrade from the [Customer Portal](/features/customer-portal/introduction) right away, while downgrades wait for renewal.

- **Product changes.** A longer billing interval (monthly to yearly) is an upgrade and a shorter one is a downgrade, whatever the prices. With the same interval, a higher or equal price is an upgrade and a lower price is a downgrade. Moving from a non-seat to a seat-based product, or from a non-unit to a unit-based product, always applies now.
- **Seat and unit changes.** Adding is an upgrade, removing is a downgrade.
- **Trials.** During a trial every change applies now, since nothing is billed until the trial ends. If the new product's trial would already be over, the trial ends and the customer is charged for the new plan.

Pass an explicit `proration_behavior` on an update to override `auto` for that change.

### Charge full amount and reset cycle (`reset`)

<Note>
  `reset` is in preview, and access is granted per organization. [Contact support](/support) to request it.
</Note>

The subscription switches to the new plan now, Polar invoices the **full price of the new plan** and the **billing cycle restarts** today. Nothing is prorated: there's no credit for the unused part of the old period and no partial charge. Future renewals follow the new anchor date.

Use it when a plan change should act like a new subscription, for example when an upgrade restarts a commitment period.

## Set the default behavior

The organization default applies to every change without an explicit `proration_behavior`, including plan changes customers make from the [Customer Portal](/features/customer-portal/introduction).

- Open **Settings → Subscriptions**
- Under **Changes**, pick a **Proration** option. It saves automatically

Over the API, it's `subscription_settings.proration_behavior` on [Update Organization](/api-reference/current/organizations/update-organization). Every field of `subscription_settings` is required, so send them all. See the [settings reference](/features/settings).

<Note>Dashboard or API only. Not available from the CLI or MCP server.</Note>

## Override per update

Every update that changes the price accepts an optional `proration_behavior` that overrides the default for that call.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    - Open the subscription
    - Click **Update Subscription**
    - On the **Plan** or **Seats** tab, make the change
    - Pick a **Proration behavior**
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    ```bash
    polar subscriptions update $SUBSCRIPTION_ID \
      --product-id $PRODUCT_ID --proration-behavior invoice
    ```

    `--proration-behavior` accepts `invoice`, `prorate`, `next_period` and `reset`.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Prompt">
    ```text Prompt
    Upgrade Jane's (jane@example.com) subscription to the Pro product and charge the prorated difference now.
    ```

    Uses the `subscriptions_update` operation of the [Polar MCP server](/integrate/mcp).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<CodeGroup>
```typescript TypeScript
await polar.subscriptions.update(subscriptionId, {

  proration_behavior: "invoice",

```


```bash cURL
curl -X PATCH https://sandbox-api.polar.sh/v1/subscriptions/$SUBSCRIPTION_ID \
```

## How the prorated amount is calculated

Polar prorates **per second**. If `S` seconds remain out of `T` in the current billing period:

- Unused credit on the old plan: `old_plan_price * S / T`
- Charge for the rest of the period on the new plan: `new_plan_price * S / T`
- Prorated difference: the new-plan charge minus the old-plan credit

An upgrade gives a positive difference, which is charged. A downgrade gives a negative one, which is credited.

Polar uses the real length of the current period, so 28-, 29-, 30- and 31-day months are exact. The time of day matters too: upgrading at noon credits a different amount than at midnight.

### Upgrade

A customer pays \$5/month on a 30-day month (2,592,000 seconds). After 1 day (2,505,600 seconds left) they upgrade to \$20/month.

- Unused credit on the \$5 plan: `$5 * 2,505,600 / 2,592,000 = $4.83`
- Charge for the rest of the period on the \$20 plan: `$20 * 2,505,600 / 2,592,000 = $19.33`
- **Prorated difference: \$14.50**

With `invoice`, \$14.50 is charged now. With `prorate`, it's added to the next monthly invoice, alongside the \$20 for the next cycle.

### Downgrade

A customer pays \$20/month on a 30-day month. After 1 day they downgrade to \$5/month.

- Unused credit on the \$20 plan: `$20 * 2,505,600 / 2,592,000 = $19.33`
- Charge for the rest of the period on the \$5 plan: `$5 * 2,505,600 / 2,592,000 = $4.83`
- **Prorated difference: -\$14.50**, a credit to the customer

With `invoice`, a \$14.50 credit invoice is issued now and added to the customer's balance. With `prorate`, the credit is applied to the next invoice.

## Downgrade credits and the customer balance

A downgrade credit is **never paid back to the card**. It goes to the customer's balance with Polar, which is applied to their next orders until it's used up.

- With `invoice`, the credit is added to the balance now.
- With `prorate`, the credit is deducted from the next invoice. Anything left over goes to the balance.

### Returning a downgrade credit to the card

To give the money back instead, [refund](/features/refunds) the **order the customer paid before the downgrade**, up to the amount they paid on it.

A refund also reduces any positive balance by up to the refunded amount. So refunding the credit moves it from the balance back to the card, and the customer isn't credited twice. With the downgrade above:

1. The customer downgrades with `invoice`, and \$14.50 goes to their balance.
2. You refund \$14.50 on the original \$20 order. Enter the amount excluding tax; Polar adds the matching tax.
3. The customer gets \$14.50 back on their card and their balance returns to zero. Their next renewal is charged the full \$5.

<Warning>
  **A refund only takes back credit that's still on the balance.** Any other proration credit stays with the customer: a `prorate` credit waiting for or already deducted from the next invoice, or balance already used by a later order. Refunding the original order in those cases pays the customer twice. Before refunding, check the customer's balance and their invoices since the change, and lower the refund by any credit that isn't on the balance.
</Warning>
