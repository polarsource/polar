**Effective date:** October 1, 2026

As your [Merchant of Record](/merchant-of-record/introduction), Polar is the legal seller of your products and is accountable to card networks, payment partners and tax authorities for every sale. The **Acceptable Use Policy** (AUP) defines what you can sell through Polar. It keeps the platform compliant and protects buyers. Your organization is checked against it when you [submit for review](/merchant-of-record/account-reviews), and Polar keeps monitoring as you sell.

<Info>
  Each category opens to show the binding policy text, why it exists and examples. Examples aren't complete lists. Polar may decline any product at its discretion, including one listed as usually fine.
</Info>

## Scope

This Acceptable Use Policy applies to all services (the “Services”) provided directly or indirectly by Polar Software, Inc. and its affiliates (“Polar”) and all Products sold by you using the Services. You are independently responsible for complying with all applicable laws in all of your actions related to your use of any such Services regardless of the purpose of the use. In addition, you must adhere to the terms of this Acceptable Use Policy. Failure to comply with this Acceptable Use Policy may result in immediate termination of your right to use any and all Services provided by Polar. Capitalized terms used but not defined herein shall have the respective meanings given to them in the Polar [Master Services Agreement](https://polar.sh/legal/master-services-terms) (the “Agreement”).

## What you can sell

Polar serves software companies, including B2B SaaS, consumer software and games. You may use the Services to sell:

- Software & SaaS;
- Digital products: Templates, eBooks, PDFs, code, icons, fonts, design assets, photos, videos, audio, and similar digital assets;
- Premium content & access: Discord server access, GitHub repository access, courses, and content requiring a subscription.

Generally, acceptable services are digital goods, software, or services that can be fulfilled by (1) Polar on your behalf (License Keys, File Downloads, GitHub or Discord invites, or private links) or (2) your site/service using our APIs to grant immediate access to digital assets or services for customers with a one-time purchase or subscription.

## Products you can't sell

Polar serves software companies (including B2B SaaS, Consumer Software, and Games). If your company’s primary offering is human services or the sale of physical goods, the Services are not designed for and should not be used by you.

You may not use the Services to sell any Product which:

1. violates any rules or regulations of Card Brand Networks;
2. violates any law or government regulation;
3. involves fraudulent, deceptive, unfair, abusive or predatory practices;
4. is marketed through the use of outbound telemarketing;
5. threatens reputational damage to Polar or any Card Brand Network;
6. results in or causes a significant risk of chargebacks, fines, damages, or harm and liability; or
7. engages in, encourages, promotes, or celebrates violence or physical harm to persons or property, or toward any group based on race, religion, disability, gender, sexual orientation, national origin, or any other immutable characteristic.

Polar reserves the right to refuse Services to sell any Product in its sole discretion.

By using the Services, you confirm that you will not accept payments with any connection to the following business categories and practices. The following list is not exhaustive. Polar reserves the right to add to it at any time, combined with placing your account under further review or suspending it in case we consider the usage deceptive, fraudulent, high-risk, or of low quality for consumers with high refund or chargeback risks. This list is determined and interpreted by Polar in its sole discretion and is subject to change:

The categories are grouped by theme below. Each one shows its item number in the policy.

## Business models

Polar is built for software and digital products that customers get right after paying. These business models don't fit that.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Physical products" id="physical-products">

> **Policy, item 1.** Physical products;

**Why:** Physical goods lead to disputes that digital products don't: items not received, not as described, lost or damaged in shipping. As the seller, Polar carries those disputes.

**Not allowed**

- Clothing and merchandise
- Printed books
- Hardware and devices

**Usually fine**

- A digital version of your product, such as an ebook or PDF

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Human services" id="human-services">

> **Policy, item 2.** Human services;

**Why:** It's hard to show that the work was delivered or meets what was promised. Delivery times are often long and open-ended.

**Not allowed**

- Consulting and coaching
- Freelance or agency development
- Products sold by the hour or project, like "Custom development"

**Usually fine**

- A fixed, clearly scoped setup or onboarding fee that belongs to your software subscription

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Donations, crowdfunding and sponsorship" id="donations-sponsorship">

> **Policy, item 3.** Donations, crowdfunding, community access, advertising, and sponsorship;

**Why:** The value is often intangible or delivered later, so it's hard to show what was bought and when it was delivered. Payments with nothing in return can also count as money transmission, which requires licenses.

**Not allowed**

- Tip jars and "buy me a coffee" payments
- Crowdfunding and pre-funding campaigns
- Ad placements and sponsorship slots

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Marketplaces" id="marketplaces">

> **Policy, item 4.** Marketplaces. Selling others' products or services using Polar against an upfront payment or with an agreed upon revenue share;

**Why:** As Merchant of Record, Polar needs a clear relationship between you and your customer. Collecting payments on behalf of third parties breaks that.

**Not allowed**

- Selling other creators' products for a revenue share
- Checkouts where buyers pay other sellers through your Polar account

**Usually fine**

- Charging for your own marketplace software, when Polar isn't used for payments between buyers and sellers

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Enabling other sellers" id="non-polar-sellers">

> **Policy, item 7.** Any product or service that enables non-Polar Sellers to sell products and services to customers;

**Why:** Your software may be fine on its own, but it moves commerce to sellers Polar hasn't onboarded. The risk is indirect but real.

**Not allowed**

- Ticketing platforms where organizers sell tickets
- Storefront builders where others sell their products

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Job boards" id="job-boards">

> **Policy, item 23.** Job boards;

**Why:** Job boards connect employers and candidates Polar can't vet. They attract scams and come with employment-law obligations.

**Not allowed**

- Job boards and paid job postings
- Recruitment and gig-work marketplaces
- Resume databases sold to employers

Hiring and resume tools are listed under [closer review](#closer-review).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Travel services" id="travel">

> **Policy, item 24.** Travel Services;

**Why:** Travel is delivered in the future through third parties, and plans change. That leads to cancellations, refunds and chargebacks.

**Not allowed**

- Flight, hotel and accommodation booking
- Travel agencies and vacation packages
- Tours, cruises and car rental booking

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Technical support and repair" id="tech-support">

> **Policy, item 34.** Technical support and repair services; or

**Why:** These are human-led and open-ended, often need remote access to a device, and lead to disputes about whether the work was done.

**Not allowed**

- Remote computer repair and virus removal
- Help desk and managed IT services
- Website maintenance services

**Usually fine**

- Software teams use to run their own help desk or IT

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Telecommunication and eSIM services" id="telecom">

> **Policy, item 29.** Telecommunication and eSIM Services;

**Why:** These products can enable spam, phishing and one-time-password abuse. They usually resell someone else's network, so Polar can't see how they're used.

**Not allowed**

- eSIMs and mobile data plans
- Virtual phone numbers
- Prepaid SMS, calling or messaging credits

**Usually fine**

- Software that helps businesses manage their messaging, such as a support inbox connected to SMS, when you don't sell the messaging capacity itself

</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Content and intellectual property

You can only sell what you own or are licensed to sell. These categories cover products that use, copy or unlock someone else's work.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Intellectual property infringement" id="ip-infringement">

> **Policy, item 5.** Any product or service that infringes upon, or enables the infringement of, the intellectual property rights of another party or that you do not own or do not have a license to;

**Why:** Infringement is illegal, and as the seller of record Polar is exposed to it directly.

**Not allowed**

- Courses, assets or software you don't own or license
- Re-uploads of other people's paid content
- Products using brands or characters without a license

**Usually fine**

- Your own work, or work you have a license to sell. Polar may ask to see the license.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reselling software licenses" id="license-reselling">

> **Policy, item 17.** Reselling software licenses without authorization;

**Why:** Customers should get valid licenses from legitimate sources. Unauthorized resale usually breaks the original provider's terms.

**Not allowed**

- Keys from unauthorized or gray-market sources
- Keys meant for another customer or region
- Shared access to third-party AI accounts or subscriptions
- AI routing or gateway services without provider agreements

**Usually fine**

- Reselling or giving access to third-party software or models with a written agreement that allows it. Polar may ask for a copy during review or later.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Getting around other services' rules" id="circumvention">

> **Policy, item 18.** Services to circumvent the rules, paywalls or terms of other services;

**Why:** These products exist to bypass restrictions someone else put in place, which creates legal and fraud risk.

**Not allowed**

- Paywall bypass tools
- Subscription or account sharing
- Tools that get around API restrictions or usage limits

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Content downloaders" id="content-downloaders">

> **Policy, item 30.** Third-party content downloaders, including, but not limited to YouTube, Instagram and Snapchat;

**Why:** Downloading from these platforms usually breaks their terms and the rights of the people who made the content.

**Not allowed**

- Video and audio downloaders
- Tools that save posts, stories or streams

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="IPTV" id="iptv">

> **Policy, item 25.** IPTV services, including software or platforms that enable IPTV service delivery;

**Why:** Polar can't verify that the channels and content are licensed.

**Not allowed**

- IPTV subscriptions and reseller programs
- IPTV panel, middleware or management software

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Real exam questions" id="exam-content">

> **Policy, item 35.** Standardized test prep platforms reselling real or past exam questions (e.g. IELTS, SAT, GMAT). Due to the high risk of copyright infringement and illegal distribution of proprietary exam content, we disallow this category entirely.

**Why:** Exam content belongs to the testing organizations. Polar can't verify that it was obtained or licensed lawfully.

**Not allowed**

- Leaked questions and "real exam" dumps
- Taking a test on someone else's behalf
- Guaranteed scores, certificates or results

**Usually fine**

- Test prep courses and study tools
- Practice questions you wrote yourself

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Cheating" id="cheating">

> **Policy, item 22.** Cheating, including but not limited to macros, cheat codes and hacks;

**Why:** These products undermine the rules of a game or platform and lead to bans, fraud and disputes.

**Not allowed**

- Game cheats, hacks and exploit tools
- Macros and bots that automate gameplay
- Rank or account boosting
- Anti-cheat bypasses

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Trademark removal" id="trademark-removal">

> **Policy, item 28.** Third-party trademark removal services;

**Why:** Removing marks or ownership notices from content you don't own makes infringement easier.

**Not allowed**

- Services that remove logos or watermarks from other people's content

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Infringing or explicit AI content" id="ai-content">

> **Policy, item 33.** Content generation that infringes on trademarks or copyrights, enables face swaps or deep fakes, or produces adult content;

**Why:** Polar can't verify consent or rights for generated content.

**Not allowed**

- Generating copyrighted characters, logos or branded content
- Face swaps and deepfakes
- Cloning the voice of a real person
- Adult image, video or audio generation

**Usually fine**

- General-purpose AI tools with safeguards that aren't marketed for these uses

AI content generation also needs a [closer review](#closer-review).

</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Data and security

These categories cover products that collect personal data, reach people without consent, or access systems they shouldn't.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Unauthorized access to data" id="unauthorized-access">

> **Policy, item 6.** Any product or service that enables unauthorized access to data belonging to another party;

**Why:** Unauthorized access is often illegal and puts the people behind the data at risk.

**Not allowed**

- Tools that bypass logins or paywalls
- "Login as" services
- Sale of leaked databases

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reselling customer data" id="customer-data">

> **Policy, item 13.** Reselling or distributing customer data;

**Why:** People don't expect their personal information to be sold on. It creates privacy and regulatory risk.

**Not allowed**

- Contact, email or phone lists
- Lead and prospect databases
- Data broker and audience profiling services

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="OSINT and people search" id="osint">

> **Policy, item 36.** Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) platforms: Services that aggregate, search, or expose personal data about individuals using public or leaked sources.

**Why:** These tools enable doxxing, stalking and harassment. Polar can't verify where the data came from or how it will be used.

**Not allowed**

- People-search and personal lookup services
- Reverse email or phone lookup
- Background checks from scraped data
- Services built on leaked or breached data

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Viruses and spyware" id="malware">

> **Policy, item 26.** Viruses and Spyware;

**Why:** Malware harms customers and third parties directly.

**Not allowed**

- Viruses, trojans and remote-access tools
- Spyware, stalkerware and keyloggers
- Credential or information stealers
- Software that monitors people without their knowledge

**Usually fine**

- Security and device management software where the people being monitored know about it

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="API and IP cloaking" id="cloaking">

> **Policy, item 27.** API and IP cloaking services;

**Why:** Cloaking hides where traffic comes from or what a service really does. It's mostly used to get around detection and platform rules.

**Not allowed**

- Services that hide the origin of traffic to avoid blocks
- Link or page cloakers that show reviewers different content

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Advertising and unsolicited outreach" id="outreach">

> **Policy, item 8.** Advertising and unsolicited marketing services, including, but not limited to, lead generation, bulk SMS and automated outreach;

**Why:** These services carry spam and consent risk, and their results are hard to verify.

**Not allowed**

- Lead generation services
- Bulk SMS platforms
- Cold email and automated LinkedIn outreach

**Usually fine**

- Email and messaging tools for people who opted in, such as newsletters

Marketing services and tools are listed under [closer review](#closer-review).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Scraping and data extraction" id="scraping">

Scraping isn't prohibited on its own. What matters is the data you collect, how you access it and what it's used for.

**Why:** Data extraction has real uses in research and analytics. It's not allowed when it leads to another category on this page or in the policy.

**Not allowed**

- Downloading copyrighted media or paid content
- Collecting contact details for outreach or lead lists
- Building profiles of individuals
- Getting around logins, paywalls or bot protection

**Usually fine**

- Exporting public tables to CSV or a spreadsheet
- Extracting public product listings, prices or specifications
- Organizing public data for research or analytics

</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Consumer trust

Customers should get what they were promised. These categories cover products that mislead or under-deliver.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Low-quality or counterfeit products" id="low-quality">

> **Policy, item 14.** Low-quality or counterfeit products or services;

**Why:** They lead to refunds, chargebacks and complaints, and often infringe someone else's rights.

**Not allowed**

- Counterfeit or replica products
- Pirated goods and fake license keys
- Products that differ from their description
- Low-effort products with little delivered value

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Fake reviews and social proof" id="fake-reviews">

> **Policy, item 15.** Fake testimonials, reviews, social proof, and review inflation platforms;

**Why:** These services mislead people about a product's quality or popularity.

**Not allowed**

- Buying or selling reviews
- Followers, likes, views or engagement
- Review exchanges
- Guaranteed ratings or rankings

**Usually fine**

- Tools that ask real customers for feedback or reviews
- Surveys and reputation management that don't fake or buy reviews

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="&quot;Get rich&quot; schemes" id="get-rich">

> **Policy, item 20.** “Get Rich” schemes or content;

**Why:** Promises of easy money lead to complaints, refunds and claims that can't be backed up.

**Not allowed**

- "Get rich quick" or passive-income courses
- Guaranteed income or trading profits
- Programs built on recruiting others

**Usually fine**

- Business or finance courses that make realistic claims. Testimonials are fine. Guaranteed outcomes aren't.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pseudo-science" id="pseudo-science">

> **Policy, item 31.** Digital services associated with pseudo-science including, but not limited to, clairvoyance, horoscopes, fortune-telling;

**Why:** Their claims can't be checked, which leads to disputes about whether the product delivered what it promised.

**Not allowed**

- Fortune-telling and psychic readings
- Personal horoscope readings

</Accordion>
