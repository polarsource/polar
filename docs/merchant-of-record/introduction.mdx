A **Merchant of Record (MoR)** is the business that legally sells your product to your customers. As your MoR, Polar resells your software, so it's Polar's job, not yours, to collect sales tax and VAT and pay it to tax authorities around the world.

You keep the relationship with your customers. Polar handles the liability.

## Payment providers vs. Merchants of Record

A **payment service provider (PSP)**, like Stripe, moves money between your customers and you. You're the seller, so every obligation that comes with a sale is yours.

A **Merchant of Record** sits one layer above. Polar is built on Stripe, and adds products, subscriptions, benefits and customer management on top, while taking on the seller's obligations.

| | Payment provider (e.g. Stripe) | Merchant of Record (Polar) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who is the seller** | You | Polar |
| **Sales tax and VAT** | You register, collect, file and pay | Polar does it |
| **APIs** | Low-level and flexible | Built for billing, with less to integrate |
| **Fees** | Lower | Higher, covering tax and compliance |
| **Pricing models** | Anything you can build | Polar's [pricing models](/concepts/products#price-types) |

### Which should you choose?

**Choose a payment provider if** you've already integrated one, want full control over low-level APIs, want the lowest fees, and are prepared to handle international taxes yourself.

**Choose Polar if** you want billing, checkout, [benefits](/concepts/benefits) and customer management ready to use, and want someone else to handle international taxes.

Both are good choices. Ship with whatever gets you there.

## How sales tax works

Most countries and many US states tax digital goods and services: VAT, GST, US sales tax and more. That applies whether or not the seller lives there.

Take a $10/month subscription sold to Sweden, where VAT is 25%:

- A Swedish **consumer** pays $12.50/month. $2.50 goes to the Swedish tax authority.
- A Swedish **business** with a VAT number pays $10/month, and accounts for the VAT itself (reverse charge).

The seller is responsible for two things:

1. **Collecting.** Charging the right tax for each customer's location and business status. Polar uses Stripe Tax for this.
2. **Remitting.** Registering with each tax authority, then filing and paying on time. Stripe Tax doesn't do this, so with a payment provider it's up to you.

Every jurisdiction sets its own rules. The UK and EU require foreign sellers to register before their first sale, while Texas only requires it above $500,000 in sales. Rates and rules on what's taxable vary too. Complying everywhere from day one is hard and expensive, even for large companies.

### Trade-offs

With Polar as your MoR:

- Polar is liable for registering, collecting, filing and paying sales tax worldwide.
- You can sell to EU businesses with reverse-charge VAT without registering for VAT yourself.
- Polar's total sales volume counts toward each country's threshold, not yours. Some customers pay tax that they wouldn't if you sold directly while under a threshold.
- You can't deduct the VAT you pay on purchases against these sales, since you aren't the seller.

<Warning>
  Polar handles sales tax, such as VAT, GST and US sales tax. You're still responsible for income tax on your earnings in your own country.
</Warning>

## Polar's coverage

Polar is registered in jurisdictions around the world and works with global accounting firms. They monitor sales volumes, register in new markets as thresholds are reached, and file and pay on an ongoing basis. You don't need to track where you owe tax, or when.

**Finance → Taxes** in your dashboard shows the tax Polar remitted for you, per jurisdiction. See [Taxes](/features/finance/taxes).

Selling enough that handling tax yourself is worth the cost? [Contact support](/support) and Polar can introduce you to the accounting firms it works with.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why doesn't my accounting software recognize Polar's EU OSS VAT number (EU372061545)?">
    OSS VAT numbers use the `EU` prefix instead of a country prefix (like `IE` for Ireland). Some
    accounting software predates the OSS program or doesn't support this format. Enter the number
    manually if your software lets you override validation, or ask your software vendor to add OSS
    support.
  </Accordion>
