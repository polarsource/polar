---

**Date**: 2026-09-16

## Context

Void configs (reducers, meters, entitlements, products) are declared in code and pushed
to Polar. The port from the standalone Void repository carried three overlapping ways of
telling "which pricing is this": a hash-based version, an incrementing generation per
product or meter, and a branch field on meters. Generation could only advance through a
raw write endpoint, since any change to the config changes its hash, and branch was an
abandoned earlier attempt. Meanwhile, nothing tied "the configuration customers are billed
under" to the organization having passed review.

## Decision

A configuration's identity is the SHA-256 of its normalized content, computed by the
server. Every meter and product row carries the version that declared it, so a product,
its subscriptions and its meters can be checked against one hash. A deployment is one row
per version, in chronological order, with a status of `draft`, `active` or `archived`. At
most one deployment per organization is active, and it is what production serves;
activating another archives the current one. Activation requires an organization that may
accept payments. The deploy endpoint is the only writer of meters and products.

## Consequences

- Version, deployment and lineage live in one table (`void_deployments`); there are no
  generation counters, branch fields, archive timestamps or organization-level default
  version settings.
- A new version creates its own meter and product rows even for unchanged definitions.
  Lookups are equality on the hash; subscriptions pin the row they were sold under.
- Pushing an identical configuration returns the existing deployment instead of a new one.
- Rollback is activating an earlier deployment again. Drafts can be exercised by passing
  `version_id` explicitly; nothing sells from a draft.
- Merchants who have not passed review can deploy and test but cannot activate.

## Alternatives considered

- **Content-addressed definitions with a version membership table**: fewer duplicate rows,
  one more table and join on every read. Not worth it at this scale.
- **Keeping generations as a lineage counter**: redundant with the deployment table's
  chronological order, and only reachable through raw endpoints that bypassed deploys.
- **Gating on `status == ACTIVE` only**: Polar lets organizations under review or snoozed
  accept payments, so the payments capability is the consistent gate.
