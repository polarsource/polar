---
title: "ADR-0013: Daily scheduled jobs run staggered in a 04:00–05:00 UTC maintenance window"
description: "Every daily cron task gets its own minute between 04:00 and 04:59 UTC, recorded in the scheduled jobs registry."
---

<Info>
**Status**: Accepted

**Area**: Backend

**Date**: 2026-09-21
</Info>

## Context

Daily cron tasks were declared independently, each one picking its own schedule, and almost
every one of them picked `CronTrigger(hour=0, minute=0)` — the obvious default. Fourteen daily
jobs therefore fired at the same instant, most of them bulk `DELETE`s over session, token and
event tables. That put a spike on the primary database that nothing in our traffic caused: we
made it ourselves, and it grew every time someone added another "prune expired rows" task.

Midnight UTC is also not a quiet moment for us. 04:00–05:00 UTC is our lowest-traffic hour —
not by a wide margin, but consistently the trough.

## Decision

Daily scheduled tasks run in a **maintenance window: 04:00–04:59 UTC**, staggered one job per
slot, four minutes apart. Declare them with `CronTrigger.from_crontab("<minute> 4 * * *")`.

When you add a daily task, take the next free slot, and add a row for it to
[Scheduled jobs](/engineering/backend-development/scheduled-jobs). No two daily jobs share a
minute.

```python
@actor(
    actor_name="external_event.prune",
    cron_trigger=CronTrigger.from_crontab("36 4 * * *"),
    priority=TaskPriority.LOW,
    max_retries=0,
)
```

This covers *daily* jobs only. Hourly and sub-hourly tasks keep their own schedules: they are
already spread across the hour, and pinning them to the window would defeat the point of
running them frequently.

## Consequences

- The database sees fourteen small maintenance jobs spread over an hour instead of one
  simultaneous burst, during the hour when it has the most headroom to absorb them.
- Every daily job has a known minute, so an incident at 04:2x can be traced to a specific task
  without reading the scheduler.
- The registry is the source of truth for "what runs when" and has to be updated in the same PR
  that adds or moves a job. A stale table is worse than no table.
- Slots are a finite resource: fifteen fit at four-minute spacing. When the window fills up,
  tighten the spacing rather than spilling out of the window — the crowding this ADR prevents is
  simultaneity, not proximity.
- A job that runs long can overlap the next slot. That is acceptable for the pruning work we run
  today; a daily job heavy enough to need exclusive use of the window should say so in the
  registry and claim the slots around it.
- Anything genuinely time-of-day sensitive (a report a human reads in the morning, a job that
  must close a financial day) does not belong in the window and should not be forced into it.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave them all at midnight**: the status quo. It is one line shorter per task and costs us a
  self-inflicted spike on the hour we least want one.
- **Spread jobs across the whole day**: minimises peak load best, but it means daily maintenance
  can hit any hour, including peak traffic, and there is no window to reason about during an
  incident.
- **Compute the slot automatically from a hash of the actor name**: no registry to maintain and
  no collisions to police by hand, but the schedule stops being readable at the call site and
  silently reshuffles when an actor is renamed.
- **A single "daily maintenance" task that calls each job in sequence**: one slot, guaranteed
  no overlap, but it couples unrelated modules and one failure stalls everything behind it.
