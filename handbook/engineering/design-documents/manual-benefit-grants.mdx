## Summary

Let merchants give a customer a benefit directly, without a subscription or an order behind it.
An expiry date and a note about why are both optional. Today every benefit a customer has is a
side effect of a purchase, and there is no supported way to say "just give this person access".

This adds a `ManualGrant` entity as a third owner of benefit grants, alongside subscriptions and
orders. It also adds two API endpoints and a Benefits tab on the customer page in the dashboard.

## Goals

- Grant one or more benefits to a customer from the dashboard or the API, with no purchase.
- Optionally set an expiry. When it passes, the grants are revoked automatically.
- Optionally record a reason, visible to anyone looking at the customer later.
- Revoke a manual grant at any time, per benefit.
- Never disturb grants that came from a purchase. A manual grant and a subscription grant for the
  same benefit must coexist and be revoked independently.

## Non-Goals

- Granting products. This is benefit-level only. Handing someone a full product is a different
  problem (pricing, orders, invoices) and belongs to a complimentary-subscription feature.
- Manual grants for every benefit type in the first version. See
  [Which benefits can be granted](#which-benefits-can-be-granted).
- Editing a manual grant after creation. Change of mind means revoke and re-grant.
- Bulk grants across many customers, or grant templates.

## Why

Support and sales ask for this constantly, and today the workarounds are all bad:

- **Comping a customer.** A refund edge case, a goodwill gesture, an early-access user, a
  conference giveaway. Right now this means creating a fake $0 product or a 100% discount code and
  pushing the customer through a checkout.
- **Fixing a broken grant.** A benefit failed to apply and the merchant wants to re-apply it
  without touching the subscription.
- **Time-boxed access to a single benefit.** "Give them the Discord role for 30 days" currently
  requires an entire trial product.

All of these are the same shape. This customer should have this benefit, because I said so, maybe
until this date. That shape has no home in the data model, so it gets faked with billing objects,
which then pollute revenue metrics and confuse the customer.

## `ManualGrant` becomes the third scope

```
Subscription ──┐
Order ─────────┼──> BenefitGrant (customer, benefit, member, properties)
ManualGrant ───┘
```

```python
class ManualGrant(RecordModel):
    customer_id: UUID
    reason: str | None
    expires_at: datetime | None
    scheduler_locked_at: datetime | None
```

`BenefitGrant` gains a nullable `manual_grant_id`. The scope composite grows from two columns to
three, and `BenefitGrantScope` / `BenefitGrantScopeArgs` each gain one key.

### Why a scope rather than a flag

A manual grant needs everything a scope provides. Its own identity, so it can coexist with a
subscription grant for the same benefit. Its own lifecycle, for expiry and independent revocation.
And it is provenance, because the reason lives on it. Needing all three is what being a scope
means.

A boolean `is_manual` with `expires_at` and `reason` on `benefit_grants` would be less code, but
it gives up all three. Uniqueness becomes a special case, nothing owns the lifecycle so expiry
needs its own dispatcher, and the reason and expiry get written once per benefit instead of once
per decision.

Being a scope also means the existing plumbing carries it. The task actors already take
`**scope: Unpack[BenefitGrantScopeArgs]`, so nothing in the grant and revoke path needs a new
signature. The cost is one extra table and one join.

### The unit of intent

**A `ManualGrant` is one act of granting.** A merchant picks a customer, ticks three benefits, sets
one expiry, writes one reason. That is one row and three children. Wanting different expiries per
benefit means creating separate manual grants, which is honest, because those are separate
decisions.

**Revocation is per child grant, not per manual grant.** Revoke one benefit and the siblings are
untouched. The parent remains as the record of what happened. Expiry, in contrast, applies to all
remaining children at once. Granting is a decision about a set. Revoking is almost always a
correction about a single benefit.

## Which benefits can be granted

The first version allows `feature_flag`, `custom` and `license_keys`. Everything else is rejected
with a validation error, enforced by one constant (`MANUALLY_GRANTABLE_BENEFIT_TYPES`) mirrored in
the frontend so the picker can't build a rejected request.

This is a scoping decision, not a permanent rule. Those three need no extra thought, since two
have no external side effect and license keys already grant and revoke per grant, so starting
there keeps the first version small. `meter_credit` is the obvious next one and probably the most
useful. Adding a type is a one-line change to the constant plus tests.

The types with customer-keyed external state, Discord roles being the clearest, need a decision
first about what happens when a manual grant and a subscription grant both claim the same role.
That is work to do before adding them rather than a reason they can't be added.

## Lifecycle

### Identity

Two rules, both enforced by the database rather than the service, because a create-time check
races with a concurrent request. The existing scope unique index gains `manual_grant_id`, so a
manual grant and a purchase grant for the same benefit are separate rows. A new partial unique
index allows at most one active manual grant per customer and benefit, limited to unrevoked rows
so revoke-then-regrant still works.

Both are built concurrently, since `benefit_grants` is too large and too hot to lock. See
ADR-0006.

### Granting

Granting takes a customer, a set of benefits, and the optional expiry and reason. Validation is
all-or-nothing. If any benefit in the request is invalid, nothing is created and the response
lists every problem by position, because partial success would leave the merchant guessing which
of their three benefits landed.

The grants come back pending rather than granted. Applying a benefit can be slow and can fail, so
it runs through the same worker path as every other grant, which means manual grants inherit the
same retries, error recording and webhooks.

### Revoking

Revoking targets a single grant, and only a manual one. A grant that came from a purchase is not
revocable this way, since a subscription's grants belong to the subscription.

Revocation is terminal. Once a manual grant is revoked it stays revoked, even if a grant job for
it is still in flight. Purchase grants behave the opposite way, because renewal and reactivation
legitimately re-grant them, but a manual grant has no renewal, so anything that re-granted it
would be a bug.

### Expiry

An expiry is not a scheduled job, it is a column. The scheduler reads `expires_at` off the row to
work out what is due, so there is no job table to keep in sync with the grants themselves and
nothing to clean up when a grant is revoked early. Subscription state changes are already
scheduled this way.

Dispatch happens once per manual grant, claimed atomically so two schedulers can't both fire it.
The expiry job then only enqueues revocations, one per benefit still active, rather than revoking
anything itself. One benefit failing to revoke does not hold up the rest, and retries stay the
revoke jobs' own business.

## What comes for free

Because manual grants are ordinary `BenefitGrant` rows with a different owner:

- **Renewal cycles skip them.** Cycling is driven by the subscription, so manual grants are not
  in scope for it. No exclusion logic was needed, and a regression test pins it.
- **The customer portal shows them**, with no product attached rather than being filtered out.
  Benefit visibility still applies, so a non-public benefit stays hidden.
- **Webhooks and system events fire** as they do for any other grant, so integrations see manual
  grants with no changes.

One thing had to be added: provenance on the read model. `BenefitGrant` gains a nullable
`manual_grant` object (`id`, `reason`, `expires_at`), nested rather than a flat `manual_grant_id`,
so a client sees why a grant exists and when it ends without a second request.
`manual_grant !== null` doubles as the "was this manual" flag.

## API surface

Both endpoints live on the existing `/v1/benefit-grants` resource rather than a new
`/v1/manual-grants` one. What merchants list and revoke is the grant. The `ManualGrant` is
internal bookkeeping that happens to hold the reason and the expiry, and exposing it as its own
resource would mean managing two objects to do one thing.

**`POST /v1/benefit-grants/`** returns `201` with the created grants, pending.

```json
{
  "customer_id": "...",
  "benefit_ids": ["...", "..."],
  "expires_at": "2026-09-01T00:00:00Z",
  "reason": "Conference giveaway"
}
```

`expires_at` is validated as a future datetime, `benefit_ids` is capped at 100, and `reason` at
500 characters.

**`DELETE /v1/benefit-grants/{id}`** returns `202` with the grant.

Both require the `benefits:write` scope. Reads stay on `benefits:read`.

## Dashboard

The customer page's old "Benefit Grants" table was a read-only block at the bottom of the Overview
tab. It becomes its own **Benefits** tab, with:

- A **Source** column showing `Manual`, with reason and expiry in a tooltip, or a link to the
  subscription or order that produced the grant. A merchant can then see at a glance what is paid
  for and what was given away.
- A **Grant benefits** button opening a modal to pick benefits, optionally set an expiry, and
  optionally write a reason. The picker only offers grantable types and hides benefits the
  customer already holds manually, so the merchant can't build a request the API will reject.
- **Revoke** on manual grants only, behind an inline confirm. When the customer would keep access
  through an active purchase anyway, the confirm says so. Otherwise revoking looks more
  destructive than it is.
- **Polling** while any manual grant is still pending, so rows settle to granted or failed without
  a manual refresh.

## Testing

Beyond the usual create and validation coverage, the tests that matter are the ones pinning the
invariants above. A manual grant and a subscription grant coexisting for the same benefit,
including license keys where each grant owns its own key. A grant job not overriding a revoked
manual grant. Manual grants not cycling on renewal. And the scheduler's eligibility query.

## Risks and open questions

- **No record of who granted.** `reason` is free text, and we don't store the acting user. Worth
  adding if this is ever used for anything contested. It is a column on `ManualGrant`, so it is
  cheap later.
- **No notification to the customer, and no warning before expiry.** A manually granted benefit
  appears silently in their portal and disappears at `expires_at` with no heads-up to either side.
